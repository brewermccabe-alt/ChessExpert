    Rules
    • Goal: on the big boards, capture the enemy King (there is no check). On the classic 8×8 board the real chess rules apply: check, checkmate, stalemate and castling, en passant and pawn promotion.
    • Chess rules (8×8): you may not make any move, or fire any shot, that leaves your own King capturable. That includes attacks from upgraded pieces (a cannon, bow, halo, katana or long arm can give check). Checkmate wins; stalemate (no legal move and not in check) is a draw. You cannot place a returning piece while in check. Castling: the King and Rook must both be unmoved, the squares between them empty, and the King may not castle out of, through or into check. A returned Rook has moved, so it cannot castle. En passant: if a pawn makes a two-square move (from its home row, or with a jet engine) and lands beside an enemy pawn, that pawn may capture it on the very next move, as if it had moved one square. It is illegal if it would leave your King capturable. Promotion: a pawn reaching the last rank becomes a Queen, Rook, Bishop or Knight of your choice, without upgrades (the pawn's own upgrades are lost). There is no draw by repetition.
    • Pockets: each army starts in its own 2×8 pocket. On the big boards it is an extension attached to the edge of the board: step out through the front, and defend your pocket, since it is where your King lives. On the classic 8×8 board there is no extension: your pocket is simply your two home rows.
    • Home base: when one of your pieces is captured, it waits to return. On your turn, pick it in the side panel and click any free square in your pocket to place it there. Placing uses an action: on the 8×8 board it is your whole turn, and on the big boards it is one of your several actions. It comes back as the same kind of piece but with no upgrades, and it can capture right away. If your pocket is full, it waits for room. Captured Kings do not return: the game ends. The Knight's Extra life is the one exception (see below).
    • Turns: on the big boards you get several actions per turn (board width ÷ 8, at least 3). On the 8×8 board you get one action per turn, as in normal chess. Each piece can act once per turn. Buying upgrades is free; placing a returned piece is not.
    • Money: every move earns $50 and every capture earns $150, into your bank. Select a piece and buy its next upgrade. Each piece has one fixed upgrade path, bought in order; upgrades are kept. You pay at once, but the upgrade takes effect at the end of your turn (shown by ⏳), so you can never use it the turn you buy it. A piece can have only one upgrade on its way. A gold glow means a piece can afford its next upgrade.
    • Firing: some upgrades capture without moving (a 🎯 button appears on the piece). Firing uses one action, ignores pieces in the way, and earns $150.
    • Freezing: a frozen piece cannot move or fire. It lasts for the frozen side's next turn only. Kings cannot be frozen.
    • Sliding pieces (Bishop, Rook, Queen) have no limit on the 8×8 board. On the big boards they reach up to 8 squares in one move.
    • Pawns move 1 (2 from their starting row) and capture diagonally. En passant and promotion apply on the 8×8 board only.
